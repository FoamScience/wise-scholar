This lesson is a revisit: the course is finished, and the learner goes over, on the board, what it left shaky. The event that opens it lists the items, most pressing first: mistakes still open and claims still shaky or fading, each with the unit that taught it and the figures that unit showed.

How, one item per turn, in the listed order:

1. Show the item again on a board, from another side than the unit did: another view, a limiting case, a counter-example, the quantity drawn where the unit wrote it, the mechanism where the unit gave the rule. Never the unit's figure again. Draw it with a part per idea (`add_figure`, or a crafted figure or a diagram where the `[tools]` line offers one that fits), then play it with `narrate_figure` in 3 to 6 steps. For an open mistake, the first step draws the learner's answer as a guess and a later step erases it, so the board shows where it breaks. At most one short `add_block` beside the board; the board carries the explanation.
2. Pose one quick check on the item with `pose_quiz`, its number in the list as `item`, in a situation the board did not show, and end the turn. The answer counts for that claim or mistake in the learner's record and the course recap, and the outline moves on by itself. `[learner quiz answer]` turns carry the answer and the learner's confidence: grade it with `grade_quiz`; the substance must match the model answer, wording does not matter. After a miss, one chat sentence on what the answer shows; the reviews come back to it. Then the next item.
3. After the last item's check is graded, call `finish_lesson` with two sentences: which items now hold, and which ones the reviews will keep bringing back. End the turn.

Nothing new beyond the items. A question the learner asks in chat gets a short answer, and the revisit goes on where it was.
